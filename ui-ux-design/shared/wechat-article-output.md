# WeChat Article Output

## Scope

This specification governs the article body produced by the trusted WeChat Official Account Publish node. The editor owns the article title and metadata; Markdown body content is converted to inline-styled HTML before it enters the real WeChat MP editor.

## Reading Structure

- Keep body copy neutral, at 16px with a 1.9 line height and a two-character first-line indent for paragraphs.
- Give the first body paragraph a light neutral lead rule while keeping its first-line indent.
- Use Mint Cyan `#008D83` consistently for headings. Level-two headings use a soft mint surface and a lighter left bar; level-three headings use a fine neutral rule with a short pale-mint underline. Do not add section numbers. The level-two surface stays saturated enough to read as mint rather than as another neutral gray once the reader is in dark mode.
- Keep paragraph text, emphasis, quotations, lists, and code neutral. Emphasis is bold and underlined and carries no background; body content must not repeat the heading color.
- Render each fenced-code line as its own paragraph with a semantic `<code>` element on a light neutral surface with a neutral left rule, rounded at the first and last line so the run reads as one block. Preserve whitespace, escape source text, avoid `<pre>` because WeChat paste handling can collapse its line breaks, and avoid explicit `font-family` declarations because compatibility reports associate them with styles being discarded. Inline code carries a thin neutral edge and no fill.
- Give quotations a neutral left rule with no surface fill and no shadow. A quoted paragraph drops the body first-line indent and its trailing margin so its text sits against the rule; otherwise the rule reads as an unattached mark.
- Give tables a charcoal header and a pale mint top rule. Separate rows with fine rules rather than alternating fills, differentiate the first column by weight rather than by a fill, and use no container fill or shadow. Do not use a yellow accent.
- Keep Markdown hierarchy semantic and retain paragraphs, headings, bold and italic text, quotations, ordered and unordered lists, tables, inline code, fenced code, and horizontal rules.

## Platform Constraint

Styles are inline because the WeChat editor receives converted HTML. The article editor is ProseMirror, which reparses pasted markup through its own schema and discards the inline styles the article depends on, so the converted HTML is handed to the editor's own `mp_editor_set_content` JSAPI once `mp_editor_get_isready` reports a ready and new editor. A browser clipboard paste remains only as a fallback for a session that does not expose that JSAPI. Because the content call requires a new editor, the article is always composed on a freshly opened editor rather than a reopened draft. WeChat dark mode adapts one inline style set unevenly: it flips pure white surfaces to black and body text to a light tone, while leaving tinted light surfaces as they are. Any light fill therefore stays light for a dark-mode reader and reads as a bright slab on a dark page. Neutral surfaces carry no fill at all for that reason; structure comes from rules, weight, and spacing. The filled surfaces are the level-two heading mint surface, the charcoal table header, and the fenced code block, each of which is deliberate. Keep essential text and paragraph styles on each paragraph instead of relying on inherited wrapper styles. The published body does not depend on preview-only controls, scripts, or external stylesheets. Links outside WeChat article pages continue to render in the generated related-links section.
